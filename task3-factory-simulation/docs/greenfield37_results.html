Mixed pallets use all three accepted shades and prohibit runs of three equal neighbouring shades along a grid axis. They do not impose an exact one-third recipe. Pure pallets contain one accepted shade only. Product requests can change after a complete pallet; a well is not permanently tied to one recipe.

The two-Mixed/one-Warm workload blocked in the present allocator after 48,105 inspections and 66 completed pallets. Waiting colours could not finish two Mixed layers at 38/40 and 39/40 in one line. Its partial-run rate is excluded from valid capacity claims. The other three scenarios completed all 57,600 inspections.

6. How the hourly metrics are calculated

Factory window T = max(time of last complete pallet, time of last inspection).

Conforming bricks/h = complete pallets × 560 × 3,600 / T (seconds).

Complete pallets/h = complete pallets × 3,600 / T (seconds).

Current example: 84 × 560 × 3,600 / 21117.713792 = 8,019.05 conforming bricks/h.

Shipped pallets/h = pallets on departed complete customer orders × 3,600 / full replay duration. This includes logistics drain and is reported separately.

Hourly values are normalized batch averages; a run need not last exactly one hour. Startup, travel, process scheduling and batch completion affect these averages. Do not add inspection throughput, unfinished-work rates or shipping rates to the complete-pallet production figure.
